# The Four Witches of the Kindergarten

The art room, afternoon. Sunlight slants in through the big windows and the whole floor smells of paint.

Whiskle is crouched at the easel nearest the window, painting the little thing in his pot that has just sprouted. Half an hour in, the sprout has grown one leaf. Mushi sits in the middle; on his paper is a big sun with twelve smiling faces on it, and he is choosing a spot for the thirteenth.

Haide is crouched on the easel furthest in. Not in front of it. On it. Brush in his mouth, tail swaying slowly, the paper already full.

"Done." He spits out the brush, jumps down, and slaps the paper onto the floor with a paw. "Come and look. My new work."

Mushi is over at once, eyes shining. "Wow! What is it?"

Four girls in a row, and, surprisingly, a good likeness. Across the top, in wobbly paw-writing, a title.

"It's called," Haide clears his throat, "The Four Witches of the Kindergarten."

"Which four?" Mushi asks.

Whiskle doesn't turn round. Quietly: "…There are only five girls in this whole kindergarten."

"Six, surely?" Mushi counts on his fingers, seriously.

Whiskle's braid goes stiff. "…Five."

Haide ignores the whole subject. He pats the top-left corner of the painting. "Fufu, Aisha, Mimi, Ruanruan. One at a time."

---

**First, Fufu.**

"I rate her," Haide pauses, "the Witch of Greed."

Mushi: "Fufu? She'd give you half her sweets."

"Let me finish." Haide sits down. "Last month I went to the snack shop with her for a lollipop. Two pretty ladies were standing by the door, talking about how much their dogs sold for. One of them saw me and said to Fufu, 'What a handsome dog. He must be worth a fortune.'"

He stops to make sure both of them are listening.

"From that day, for most of a month, Fufu looked at me differently. She used to look at me like a friend. Now she looks at me like a savings account. Every day she asks me to the snack shop. Three times a day. Something felt off. I never went once."

Mushi strokes his chin. "Well…" He folds his arms and narrows his eyes. "This doesn't come free."

Haide stares at him.

Three seconds.

"Fine, fine, I'll tell you." Mushi deflates. "Fufu did ask me a question once. She asked how much a dog about Haide's age would go for, if you sold it."

Haide's tail stops.

"So," he says slowly, "my instincts were right."

Whiskle's brush stops on the paper for a second, then goes back to the leaf.

---

**Second, Aisha.**

"What about Aisha?" Whiskle asks, still not turning round. "What witch is she?"

"The Witch of Envy, obviously." Haide says it fast, like he decided long ago. "Have you seen that red book of hers? It's like a Death Note. I sneaked a look once and she'd written my name in it. And then I noticed a pattern: every time she writes something in that book, two days later I have bad luck. Bones go missing. Mat gets wet. Ball rolls into the drain. Is that a coincidence? It is not."

"So why does she write you down?" Mushi asks.

"Why else?" Haide lifts his chin. "She envies my brilliant mind and my dashing good looks."

Whiskle, quietly: "…You put a paw print on her book last week."

"That was an artist's signature." Haide's voice drops a little. "She's been strange the last couple of days, though. Keeps staring at me. Doesn't say anything, just stares. I'm lying there asleep, I open my eyes, and she's right there, watching. Chills down my back."

Mushi thinks. "Aisha's been very interested in anatomy lately. She's been reading those books, skeleton diagrams, organ diagrams, reading them really carefully."

He hears himself, and pauses.

"Don't know if that's related."

Haide says nothing.

Haide says nothing again.

"…"

---

**Third, Mimi.**

"And Mimi?" Whiskle asks. "What did she do to you?"

Haide's face goes very tired.

"The Witch of Wrath, obviously," he says. "Do you even need to ask?"

"What did she do?"

Haide lifts his left front paw, sets it down slowly, lifts the right one, sets it down slowly.

"Guess why I walked on three legs last week."

Silence.

Mushi, quietly: "I thought you were doing it to look cool."

"I was doing it to look cool," Haide says. "And healing."

---

**Fourth, Ruanruan.**

"The last one," Mushi is confused, "Ruanruan? A girl that soft? I've never once seen her pick on you."

"You don't understand." Haide shakes his head. "As they say: an innocent man, a guilty jade."

"What does that mean?"

"It means, whose fault is it that she's that easy to pick on?" Haide starts counting on his paw. "One, that bunny of hers is a perfect carry. Two, she puts it in the same place every single time, I could find it with my eyes shut. Three, I take it, she cries, I give it back, she smiles, and then she puts it back in the same place."

He sums up. "Clearly can't be bothered to defend against me. That's the Witch of Sloth."

Whiskle finally turns round, eyebrows drawn together. "…That's not sloth. That's trust."

"It's sloth," Haide insists. "Trust is…"

---

He doesn't finish.

From the far end of the corridor comes a howl that carries through three classrooms and a courtyard, perfectly clear:

"FOUND HIM!!! Haide's in the art room!!!"

Fufu's voice. Very excited. Excited the way you are when you've found something valuable.

Haide's ears shoot up and his tail puffs into a ball.

"Uh…"

He's gone. Brush on the floor, painting on the floor, four witches still in a row on the paper.

An orange shape flashes past the door like wind, followed by a second shout: "Don't run! I just want a quote!"

<!-- illustration:2|"Don't run! I just want a quote!" -->

The classroom goes quiet.

Mushi looks at Haide's painting on the floor, then down at his own sheet of paper.

He picks up his pen. On the paper with its sun full of smiling faces, he draws five people in witch hats: Fufu, himself, Aisha, Mimi, and Ruanruan. Last of all, in one corner, he adds a tiny dog running away.

<!-- illustration:3|Mushi's drawing: Five Witches and a Fleeing Dog -->

"This one doesn't come free," he tells himself, then adds, "…forget it, he wouldn't pay anyway."

Whiskle turns back and goes on painting his leaf.

After a while, he says quietly: "…Six."

Mushi: "What?"

"Nothing."

(End)
